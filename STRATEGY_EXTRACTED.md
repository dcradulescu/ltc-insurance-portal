# LTC Navigation Strategy - Extracted Logic

## Phase 1: Pre-Filing Claim Packet (Days 1-14)

### Step 1a - Day 1
**Task:** Call the LTC Insurance company and complete the form in the LTC Insurance Navigator App
**Logic:** Determine: Policy Information, Benefits of the Policy, Limitations and Requirements (Elimination Period, Lookback Period, Maximums, Waiver of Premium, Equipment Allowance, etc.), Have the Claim Packet MAILED to you.

### Step 2 - Day 1-14
**Task:** Claim Packet mailed to insured - NOT Filed On The Phone
**Logic:** Waiting period for claim packet arrival

### Step 3 - Day 2-3
**Task:** Equipment Allowance - If you need equipment, you should acquire that equipment immediately!
**Logic:** If there is an equipment allowance, and you need equipment, coordinate the install or acquisition of that equipment.

### Step 4a - Day 3-9
**Task:** Secure Home Care Company
**Logic:** While waiting for the Claim Packet to arrive:
- The provider needs to have experience filing claims with your LTC Insurance company
- Present insured with Plan of Care
- State License and Liability Insurance declaration sheet
- These 3 documents need to be sent to the LTC Insurance company as part of the Claim Packet
- Plan of Care NEEDS to match what is going to be stated in the claim form
- Must match what is stated in the doctor's chart and letter
- Must match the same finding that will be reported in the independent nurse visit notes

### Step 4b - Day 9
**Task:** Determine AOB - If the Home Care provider accepts payment directly from the LTC Insurance company or if the LTC Insurance company pays the insured and reimburses for payments for Home Care
**Logic:** If so, have the Home Care provider complete the AOB form

### Step 4c - Day 10-90
**Task:** Documentation - ADL's needed and / or Substantial Supervision needed by the care staff, documented as Care Notes
**Logic:** Must match the minimum requirements of the policy. Care Notes need to be matched to the invoices and sent in when the Claim Form is submitted. Day 90 is assuming a 90 day standard elimination period.

### Step 4d - Day 10-90
**Task:** Start Care from Home Care provider
**Logic:** Starting care shows that home care services are needed urgently. When the Third Party Independent Nurses Assessment is performed, the nurse will be asking how long care has been provided.

### Step 5 - Day 4-21
**Task:** Letter to Doctor(s) - A letter needs to be crafted to each of the doctor, specialists, etc.
**Logic:** 
- This letter needs to be signed by the doctor(s) and placed in the insured's chart/file
- Provide the Doctor(s) with a copy of the Home Care providers Plan of Care to include in their documentation as well
- The letters to the current medical providers will help to substantiate the need for long term care, based on the diagnosis, need for minimum ADL's and / or Cognitive Decline requiring substantial supervision
- A sample of the letter needs to be provided as a download in the LTC Navigator App, dynamically built based upon the insured's needs, diagnosis and prognosis if left unattended
- The letter should also include that the insured needs help during all waking hours and/or at night if the insured wakes up at night
- This is important to help define that there is a scope of time that the medical professionals have
- Internally, most LTC Insurance companies have an allowance of time per day based on the condition
- Care coordination may determine, as an example, that the care needs are approximately 4 hours à day
- Even if your policy will cover 10 hours a day, the LTC Insurance company may still need to approve the additional time, since they accounted for 4 hours internally
- Having medical professionals provide a proactive recommendation on an authorized amount of time, reduces the likelihood of an "approval" if you need to increase the amount of time that care is needed
- Yes, even if within your daily maximum with an approved claim, it will take time to create the letter, visit the providers and then wait for the signatures, and then get those back
- Best practice is to call ahead of time, then show up and wait for the letter to be signed

### Step 6 - Day 5-20
**Task:** Gather Lookback Documentation
**Logic:**
- Most lookback windows are 1 year from the time that the claim is filed
- This will be determined in the initial call
- If there are services that you have paid for out of pocket, such as extended rehabilitation stays or previous home care services, then look for those receipts or call the companies and secure documentation
- This can take some time for companies to produce the documentation needed
- Please note that any lookback services that were covered by Medicare or Medicaid are not allowed

### Step 7a - Day 10
**Task:** Gather Medical Information (Providers names address phone and fax numbers, Medications, Diagnosis, etc.)
**Logic:** You will be asked for all of your medical providers information when the Claim Packet arrives. This is a preemptive step to prepare for completing the claim packet.

### Step 7b - Day 12-13
**Task:** Create a written justification for need for the packet
**Logic:**
- Dates and times of noted medical incidents or events (recent falls, decline in stability, decline in cognition, etc.)
- Notes on general decline in the ability to perform the Activities of Daily Living that are considered the primary 6 ADL's
- Please note that the decline of ADL's is what is focused on, not necessarily the diagnosis
- Example: I fell twice, once on 1/1/26 and once on 1/4/26 DOESN'T show a decline in ADL's, it shows a situation
- You will want to indicate how that has impacted you
- Fell twice (1/1/26 and 1/4/26) resulting in a loss of balance, requiring a need for help to bath, dress and walk independently
- Again, everything is tied back to the ADL's not only the supporting diagnosis

### Step 8 - Day 14
**Task:** Change Premiums to Monthly
**Logic:**
- Premiums are most often paid in a quarterly, or annual payment
- What to know is that the insurance company will not refund your excess premium that has been paid past a claim approval
- Example: You pay premiums quarterly. You paid January through March. Your claim was approved on Feb. 1. You have paid premiums for all of February and all of March. That premium is typically not reimbursed
- This premium is held until the insured no longer needs care and premiums are required to be paid again
- If the insured receives payment from the insurance company until they pass away, the insurance company will submit that to the insured's estate
- For this reason, a lot of individuals who's health starts to decline, change their insurance premiums to monthly payments so that any extra premiums are not held indefinitely by the insurance company

## Phase 2: Claim Process (Days 15-48)

### Step 1 - Day 15-16
**Task:** Complete the Claim Packet
**Logic:** Compile all of the questions in the Claim Packet. Remember to attach ALL documents in the list provided.

### Step 2 - Day 17
**Task:** Submit the Claim Packet
**Logic:** The Claim Packet must be submitted in a single packet and not in parts.

### Step 3 - Day 18-48
**Task:** Claim Approval / Denial Window (30 days from receipt)
**Logic:** The LTC Insurance company will notify you by mail of a claim being approved or denied. Hint: You can call the Claims Dept and they will tell you ahead of time if a determination has been made.

### Step 4 - Day 18-48
**Task:** Third Party Independent Nurse Evaluation
**Logic:** A nurse will be contacting you to set up a day and time for a third-party independent evaluation. This evaluation is to determine whether or not the claim should be approved.

### Step 5 - Day 18-48
**Task:** Medical Records pulled from medical providers
**Logic:** The LTC Insurance company will contact your listed medical providers and request their medical records.

## Key Timelines Summary

- **Day 1:** Call insurance + complete form + request claim packet
- **Day 2-3:** Equipment allowance acquisition (if applicable)
- **Day 3-9:** Secure home care company
- **Day 4-21:** Get doctor letters signed
- **Day 5-20:** Gather lookback documentation
- **Day 10:** Gather medical information
- **Day 12-13:** Create written justification
- **Day 14:** Change to monthly premiums (if waiver of premium)
- **Day 15-16:** Complete claim packet
- **Day 17:** Submit claim packet
- **Day 18-48:** Insurance review (30-day window)
  - Third party nurse evaluation
  - Medical records pulled
  - Claim approval/denial determination

## Critical Success Factors

1. **Doctor Letters:** MUST be in doctor's file and signed. This is the #1 cause of claim denial.
2. **Care Notes:** Must match the Plan of Care and invoices.
3. **Plan of Care:** Must match doctor's findings and claim form statements.
4. **Consistency:** Everything must align - claim form, doctor letters, care notes, plan of care, third party evaluation.
5. **Timing:** All tasks must be completed within the 14-day pre-filing window to meet the 30-day insurance review deadline.
6. **Documentation:** All documents must be submitted together in a single packet, not in parts.
