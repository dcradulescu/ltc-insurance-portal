## VOICEOVER SCRIPT (FULL)

"Insurance policies are filled with confusing terms. What does ADL mean? What's an elimination period? How much will you actually receive? Let's break it down.

ADL stands for Activities of Daily Living. These are the basic tasks you do every day: bathing, dressing, eating, toileting, transferring, and maintaining continence. Your insurance policy typically pays benefits when you need help with two to three of these activities.

The Elimination Period is the number of days you must pay for care before your insurance starts paying. Think of it like a deductible. Common elimination periods are 30, 60, or 90 days. During this time, you're responsible for all care costs.

Your Daily Benefit Amount is the maximum your insurance will pay each day for care. For example, if your policy pays $150 per day, that's $4,500 per month. Most policies have a maximum benefit period of 3 to 5 years, though some are unlimited.

Now you understand the key terms in your policy. You know what ADLs are, how elimination periods work, and what your daily benefit means. Ready to take the next step? Let's gather your policy details so we can create your personalized strategy."
